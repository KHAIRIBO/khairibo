02 · What's working, what's leaking

The brand is ready. The infrastructure isn't.

Strengths on Instagram

  • Distinct cuisine — Mafé, Thieboudienne — that stands out in Amsterdam's food scene.
  • Follower count well above typical weekend pop-ups.
  • Strong visual identity in reels: warmth, community, the room at night.

What a website closes

  • No permanent menu — dishes live in captions and disappear.
  • Location has moved (Papaverweg → Woodies at Berlin); nothing anchors "where we are now."
  • Reservations depend on a single bio link, easy to lose or misconfigure.
  • Zero search visibility — "Senegalese food Amsterdam" won't surface the account.
  • No owned email or contact list — every follower is one algorithm change from unreachable.

09 · Build principles

Small on purpose, not by accident.

Step 1

Lock the essentials

Pull the current menu, this month's location, hours, and best reel footage from Instagram into one place. This is content you already have.

Step 2

One page, built to answer two questions

"What's on the menu" and "how do I book" above the fold, in that order. Instagram feed embedded lower down for atmosphere, not navigation.

Step 3

Own the booking

A simple reservation form that emails you directly — no dependency on a third-party bio-link tool that can vanish or change its pricing.

Step 4

Let it show up in search

Basic local SEO — name, cuisine, neighborhood — so "Senegalese food Amsterdam" finds Omoka the same week it's typed in.
